  <name>Task 2: Correct overnight sleep date attribution (G-NW-11-13)</name>
  <files>js/lib/metrics.js, js/lib/day-bucket.js</files>
  <action>Address the architectural mismatch where a late bedtime (e.g. 22:30 on 31.03) and late wake (e.g. 07:30 on 01.04) land in separate day buckets. The issue is that the bucketing rule (day-bucket.js lines 97-104) uses the cutover hour (default 04:00) to determine which date an event belongs to. A wake at 07:30 on 1.04 (>= 4 AM) stays on 1.04, but a bedtime at 22:30 on 31.03 also stays on 31.03. This splits the overnight sleep into two buckets.

Fix by modifying the date attribution logic in aggregateMetrics (metrics.js around line 163):
- Currently: `const dateStr = extractDate(day.wake) || extractDate(day.bedtime) || ...`
- Corrected: If day has a bedtime but no wake, infer that the wake occurred on the following calendar date. Modify the logic to detect this overnight case and use the bedtime date + 1 day as the row date (representing the wake date). Alternatively, pair bedtime-only records with the next calendar day's bucket to find the matching wake.

Concretely:
1. In aggregateMetrics, when row.date would fall back to bedtime-only (wake is null), check if a following bucket exists with a wake at a later calendar date. If found, attribute the row to that wake date instead.
2. Or, in the aggregation loop, when processing a day bucket with bedtime but no wake, defer or mark it for pairing with the next bucket (which should have the wake).
3. The simplest approach: when aggregating, if a day has bedtime but no wake, use bedtime's calendar date + 1 (adding 1 day) as the logical wake date, since the wake must occur after midnight.

Implement the date attribution fix in metrics.js. Ensure tests verify that a sleep spanning 31.03 (bedtime) to 1.04 (wake) is recorded with a row date of 1.04.
  </action>
